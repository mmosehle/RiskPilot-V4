Understanding the Surprise Model
What is a Surprise?
A surprise = Actual − Forecast. For indicators where a higher number is economically stronger (e.g. NFP, GDP, ISM), a positive surprise generally creates a bullish bias for USD and a bearish bias for Gold. A negative surprise reverses this.
Why Inflation is Different
For CPI and PPI, higher = USD bullish because hotter inflation implies the Fed is less likely to cut rates. Lower-than-expected inflation suggests rate cuts are more likely, which weakens the USD and supports Gold.
FOMC Nuance
The rate decision alone is rarely the full story. The statement, dot plot, and press conference often create larger moves than the decision itself. A "hawkish cut" (cutting but signalling fewer future cuts) can actually strengthen USD.
Bias ≠ Direction
A bullish USD bias means the data favours USD strength based on historical interpretation. The actual market reaction can differ — especially if the outcome was already priced in or if positioning was extreme before the release.
